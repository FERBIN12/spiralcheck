# SpiralCheck validation (synthetic data)

Generated by `node tools/validate.mjs` on 2026-11-23 with Node v24.22.3. Every number below comes from that run; nothing is hand-edited.

## Method

Each synthetic drawing is an Archimedes spiral (r = 10 px/rad * theta, 3 turns, loop spacing 62.8 px) traced at near-constant pen speed with irregular sample timing (+/-30% jitter, like real pointer events). On top of it: Gaussian position noise (0.5 px SD unless noted), a slow 0.4 Hz radial drift of 2 px (normal hand wander), and a sinusoidal tremor of known frequency and amplitude. The analyzer gets only the {x, y, t} samples and a center guess that is 6 px off.

A trial passes if the recovered tremor frequency is within 0.5 Hz of the injected one. 10 random seeds (noise, phase, timing) per cell. "Amp ratio" = estimated amplitude / injected amplitude (1.00 is perfect). "Detected" counts trials where the tremor flag was raised (amplitude >= 0.5 px and >= 40% of 3-15 Hz power near the peak).

## Summary

- Radial tremor (conditions A to C): **1080/1080** trials recovered the frequency within 0.5 Hz.
- Condition D shows the known limitation: the app measures the *radial* error, so a tremor along a fixed axis is projected onto the radius and only part of its amplitude is seen (about 2/3, see the amp ratio column). The frequency also reads about 0.3 Hz off because rotating the axis relative to the radius splits the peak into sidebands at the tremor frequency plus or minus the drawing rotation rate. It still stays inside 0.5 Hz here.
- At 0.5 px injected amplitude (the same size as the position noise) the frequency is still recovered, but the tremor flag is raised only in some trials: 0.5 px is the practical detection floor.
- These are synthetic checks of the signal processing only. They say nothing about clinical accuracy. SpiralCheck is not a medical device.
